Sarga 3 — Devatāsthāpanaḥतृतीयः सर्गः · देवतास्थापनः — नव्या भूमीत देवतांची प्रतिष्ठापना

तृतीयः सर्गः · देवतास्थापनः

The installation of deities in the new landनव्या भूमीत देवतांची प्रतिष्ठापना · 59 verses59 श्लोक

Rāvaṇa's spies Śuka and Sāraṇa, posted to gather news of the world, see the sea driven back and a new coastal land with a radiant ascetic-warrior on it, and report to Rāvaṇa (3.1–7). From their description he recognizes Paraśurāma (3.8). He fears that if the whole ocean is pushed back, Laṅkā will lose its sea-moat (3.9), so he sets out in the Puṣpaka to make peace (3.10). Flying over the exposed seabed, he searches for Paraśurāma and sees his light on a mountaintop (3.11–14). He lands at a distance, walks up humbly and finds the sage in meditation on a rock, ash-smeared, with rudrākṣa beads and matted hair (3.15–17). Rāvaṇa first meditates on Śiva himself, then bows, and Paraśurāma offers him a seat (3.18–19). Rāvaṇa praises him, claims friendship through their shared deity Śiva, and asks what has happened to the sea (3.20–22). Paraśurāma explains that, having given the whole earth to a brahmin, he took this land from the sea with his arrow, and asks why Rāvaṇa has come (3.23–24). Rāvaṇa explains that Laṅkā is safe only because of its sea-fortress; if the sea retreats that far, mighty kings could march on it by land. He asks Paraśurāma to be content with the land he has (3.25–27). Paraśurāma says तथास्तु and Rāvaṇa flies home satisfied (3.28). The divine women held captive in Laṅkā hear of Paraśurāma's greatness and rejoice (3.29). They decide he is the eternal Being born in Jamadagni's house to destroy oppressive kings, that even Rāvaṇa has sought his protection, and that he will not ignore them (3.30–33). At night they escape by a secret path to Iṣupāta and play freely on the shore, safe under his protection (3.34–35). Stung by Rāvaṇa's harsh words, bands of rākṣasas roam about to recapture them (3.36). They find the women resting at Paraśurāma's feet and drag them off with clubs and blows, and the women's cries reach the sage (3.37–38). Paraśurāma takes up bow, quiver and axe, raises one hand in अभय, and charges (3.39). His bowstring and lion-roar stun the rākṣasas; a fierce battle follows, his arrows never stop, and the rākṣasas fall like mountains whose wings have been cut (3.40–43). He offers the slain to the new land as a bali, consecrating it with their blood and their lotus-like heads (3.44). One man carries the news to Rāvaṇa (3.45). Having protected the goddesses, Paraśurāma decides the rākṣasas' crime must be punished and sets out in anger (3.46). He resolves to drown Laṅkā on Trikūṭa, with Rāvaṇa and all his allies, and grind it to dust (3.47). Brahmā, afraid for his creation, warns Rāvaṇa (3.48), while Laṅkā already spins like Kārtavīrya's weapon in a whirlwind (3.49). Brahmā brings Rāvaṇa humbly to the angry sage, lays him at Paraśurāma's feet and pleads for him: Rāvaṇa did not knowingly order the goddesses seized, his men have already been punished, and since Paraśurāma once gave him अभय, he should now forgive him (3.50–54). Paraśurāma's anger subsides; he bows to Brahmā and takes leave (3.55). He installs the rescued goddesses in his land with boons and a share of authority, and foretells that its towns, villages, forests and cattle-settlements will soon be his domain, where people will settle and worship those deities (3.56–57). People will name places after the goddesses and hold great festivals in season, and the goddesses, guarding their shrines, will fulfil their worshippers' wishes (3.58). Delighted with his boons and with their share of his peerless land, the goddesses settle there happily (3.59). The sarga's title, देवतास्थापनः ("the installation of the deities"), names this closing episode.
